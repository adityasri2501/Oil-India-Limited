# Engineering assumptions and limits

## Data provenance

The initial asset is synthetic `BGW-001`. The known Baghewala context used to bound the interface is OIL India's public description: Jodhpur Sandstone heavy crude, approximately 1,150 m average depth, reported viscosity around 10,000–13,000 cP at 50 °C, reservoir temperature around 46–48 °C, and CSS with sucker-rod pumping. The model's 47 °C initial temperature is the midpoint of that public range; it is not a measured BGW-001 reading. All per-well controls, pressures, rates, loads, cycle values and telemetry are assumed or generated. The prototype must not be presented as measured Baghewala performance.

## Screening equations

- Thermal response is a lumped heat balance with assumed soak maturation and a 60-day exponential production-cooling time constant. The displayed temperature/rate profile is a forecast generated by this formula, not measured history. The soak-maturity time constant (168 hours), production-cooling time constant (60 days), and heat balance are uncalibrated; the model omits steam quality, latent heat accounting, geology, heat loss by layer, well geometry and actual cycle history. OIL publicly describes steam at 250–320 °C, injection about 14–21 days and soak at about half the injection duration; the temperature and injection-duration context are public bounds, but the current model only uses steam temperature; soak and production duration are scenario inputs and steam mass remains a synthetic assumption.
- Steam injection pressure is captured and preserved as a user input but is not used to alter outputs or optimized. This requires a well-specific pressure/steam-quality and injectivity relationship plus approved completion/integrity limits; those are not available for the synthetic well.
- Oil viscosity uses an Andrade-style exponential temperature response anchored to 12,000 cP at 50 °C. It is not a fitted Baghewala PVT correlation.
- Mobility and inflow use a square-root viscosity ratio and fixed productivity/drawdown. No multiphase reservoir simulation or pressure-transient calibration is included.
- An implicit fixed-point calculation couples inflow and a quadratic tubing-friction pressure-loss proxy, which reduces available drawdown. It is not a multiphase pressure traverse; tubing diameter, fluid properties, gas fraction and measured pressure are not modeled.
- VFD frequency is captured for traceability but does not drive modeled SPM; unit-specific motor, gearbox, stroke and frequency calibration is not available. SPM remains the direct speed input. SRP displacement uses geometric barrel displacement. Fillage, polished-rod/minimum-load and risk are transparent empirical screening proxies, not API TR 11L design calculations or dynacard interpretation.
- Water cut and energy equations are demonstrative assumptions. SOR is steam tonnes divided by modeled oil volume over the selected production period. Risk is normalized from rod-float and load screening proxies; thermal exposure is not scored because approved well/equipment temperature limits are unavailable.
- The former synthetic-trained ML surrogate has been removed. The optimizer now uses direct simulator evaluations. The optional supervised model is trained only from uploaded measured well records and remains a separate measured-data estimate; no real well file is bundled, so it is untrained until one is supplied.
- Optimizer risk and SOR thresholds are user-adjustable exploration filters, not operating or safety limits. It evaluates the current fixed pump diameter, efficiency, production period, and recorded injection pressure, and can apply a feasible CSS/SRP recommendation back to the twin.

All equations and coefficients require calibration using approved well data and review by petroleum, artificial-lift and HSE specialists before any operational use. Outputs are for demonstration and engineering discussion only.

## Demo economics and reliability indicators

The UI now exposes energy intensity, a transparent illustrative daily cost, an impact-load index, and a relative reliability index. Demo economics use fixed example assumptions of INR 8/kWh and INR 1,200/tonne of steam; these are not Baghewala tariffs. Impact load is the normalized difference between maximum and minimum rod-load screening values. The relative reliability index is 1 minus the normalized rod-float/load screening score. Neither is a failure probability, remaining useful life estimate, nor equipment certification. Rod failure and pump-unsetting outcomes require authorized historical event labels and field calibration.
